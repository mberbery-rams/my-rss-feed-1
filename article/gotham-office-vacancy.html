Gotham City Office Vacancy Falls to Five-Year Low

Gotham Property Review · Market desk

Office vacancy across Gotham City fell to 9.4 per cent in the third quarter, the lowest level recorded since before the pandemic, according to figures published this week by the city's planning and economic development office. The rate has now declined for five consecutive quarters, down from a peak of 16.8 per cent.

Net absorption reached 1.2 million square feet over the period, driven largely by financial services and legal tenants consolidating into higher-specification buildings in the central business district. Leasing velocity in secondary stock remained subdued, and analysts cautioned that the headline figure conceals a widening gap between prime and older assets.

Prime headline rents rose 6.1 per cent year on year, with the strongest growth recorded in buildings completed after 2015 or comprehensively refurbished since. Incentive packages have shortened materially, with rent-free periods on ten-year terms falling from an average of fourteen months to nine.

Investment volumes have not yet followed the leasing recovery. Transaction activity across the city remained roughly 30 per cent below the five-year average, which brokers attribute to a continuing gap between vendor and purchaser pricing expectations rather than to any weakening in underlying occupier demand.

Several advisers expect the tightening to broaden into adjacent submarkets over the next eighteen months as options in the core become scarcer and tenants with 2027 lease events begin their search processes earlier than has been typical.
