North American Logistics Rents Climb 6% as Supply Pipeline Thins

Continental Real Assets Monitor · Research desk

Prime logistics rents across North America rose 6.2 per cent over the twelve months to the end of the third quarter, according to research published this week, as the development pipeline contracted for a sixth consecutive quarter. Completions over the period were the lowest recorded since 2016.

Researchers attributed the tightening principally to the withdrawal of speculative development finance in late 2023 and early 2024, the effects of which are only now reaching delivery. Space under construction stands at roughly 41 per cent of the level recorded at the peak, with the sharpest reductions in inland distribution corridors rather than coastal port markets.

Take-up was broadly flat year on year, so the rental growth is understood to be a function of constrained supply rather than accelerating occupier demand. Vacancy in the best-quality stock fell below 3 per cent in several markets, a level at which researchers noted tenants typically begin renewing earlier and accepting shorter incentive packages.

The report cautioned that the pipeline is expected to begin recovering from late next year as schemes that secured finance in 2025 reach delivery, and that rental growth of the magnitude recorded this year is unlikely to be sustained beyond the current cycle. Investors were advised to underwrite reversion conservatively where leases expire after 2027.
