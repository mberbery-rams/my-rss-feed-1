Norwegian Salmon Exports Climb on Strong Asian Demand

Nordic Trade Bulletin · Commodities desk

Norwegian salmon exports rose 11 per cent by volume in the third quarter, driven by stronger demand from South Korea and Japan and by a weaker krone that improved competitiveness against Chilean producers. Export value increased 14 per cent over the same period.

Producers reported average harvest weights slightly above the same quarter last year despite warmer water temperatures along parts of the western coast. Biological costs per kilogram were broadly stable, with sea lice treatment expenditure down marginally following wider adoption of mechanical delousing across the larger operators.

Industry analysts expect supply growth to moderate through the first half of next year as licence utilisation approaches practical limits in the most productive regions. Several producers have signalled capital expenditure on land-based smolt capacity rather than on additional sea sites, reflecting the constrained licensing environment.

Freight availability improved over the quarter after two years of disruption, and air cargo rates on Asian routes fell approximately 9 per cent, which exporters said had a measurable effect on landed cost into the premium retail segment. Processors noted that the share of exports leaving the country in fillet rather than whole-fish form continued its gradual multi-year rise, a trend they expect to persist as domestic processing capacity expands.
