Wayne Tower Refurbishment Completes in Gotham City

Gotham Property Review · Development desk

The comprehensive refurbishment of Wayne Tower, the 51-storey office building on Gotham City's central axis, reached practical completion this month after a twenty-eight month programme. The works covered full replacement of the facade, mechanical and electrical systems, and the creation of 24,000 square feet of amenity space across two floors.

The building is now approximately 71 per cent pre-let, with the largest commitment taken by a professional services firm relocating from two older buildings elsewhere in the district. Agents marketing the remaining floors said they expect the building to reach full occupancy during the next leasing cycle given the shortage of comparable refurbished stock in the city.

The project is one of the larger repositioning schemes completed in Gotham City in recent years and is being watched by owners of similar 1980s-era towers weighing refurbishment against redevelopment. Cost consultants involved in the scheme put the final capital expenditure at roughly $180 per square foot, above the original budget but below the level at which the owner had indicated demolition would have been the preferred route.

Energy performance improved by two ratings bands, which advisers said was material to securing the anchor tenant. Several institutional owners in the district are understood to have commissioned feasibility studies on comparable interventions following the scheme's progress.
